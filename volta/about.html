We started because a wallet should not need an app.

VOLTA began in a shared workshop in Kreuzberg in 2019, after one of us watched a card get read through a coat pocket at a market stall. The fix is not electronics. It is a layer of the right material in the right place.

Small runs, one workshop

Every wallet is cut, skived, stitched and edged in Berlin by a team of six. We make roughly four hundred pieces a month, which is the number that lets one person finish a wallet from start to end rather than passing it down a line.

It also means we can repair what we made. Send a wallet back with a split seam and it goes to the bench, not to a bin.

Two materials, chosen properly

The shell is vegetable-tanned full-grain cowhide from a tannery outside Florence that has been running the same pits since the 1950s. Vegetable tanning takes weeks instead of hours, and it is why the leather darkens rather than peels.

The shield is a copper and nylon mesh, thin enough to sit between the lining and the shell without adding a ridge you can feel through the leather.

How a wallet gets made

Four stages, one bench, roughly three hours of hand work per piece.

Cut hide selection

Panels are cut by hand around scars and stretch marks, so no two hides yield the same number of wallets.

Line shield layer

The mesh is bonded to the inner panel and trimmed past the card slots so there is no gap at the edges.

Stitch saddle seam

Two needles, one thread, locked at every hole. A cut stitch stays put instead of unravelling along the seam.

Finish edge work

Edges are sanded, burnished and waxed in three passes. This is the part that decides how the wallet ages.

The hide

We buy whole hides and lose about a fifth of each one.

A hide arrives with the life the animal had written into it: fence scars, insect marks, stretch along the belly. Corrected-grain leather sands all of that off and sprays a uniform pattern back on. We cut around it instead, which is slower and wastes more, and is the reason two Onyx wallets are never quite the same shade.

What you get for that is leather that keeps its grain, takes oil from your hands, and goes darker at the corners you touch most.

What we will not claim

A shielded wallet stops a reader reaching a card while the card is inside it. It does not protect a card you have handed to somebody, it does not encrypt anything, and it does nothing about online fraud, which is where most card theft actually happens.

We would rather say that plainly than sell a wallet on fear. If you want the detail on how we check the shielding, it is written up in the FAQ.
